2026 Summit

From June 9–11, MOMRI Research Fellows and eight of our Affiliates gathered at Queen's University Belfast in Northern Ireland for three days of in-depth discussion and exchange. Together, we reflected on existing research, outstanding questions, and opportunities to strengthen connections between scholarship and practice. The Summit also provided opportunities for exchanges with researchers and doctoral students at Queen's University Belfast, as well as representatives of Beyond Skin, a Belfast-based music and peacebuilding organization. We are so grateful to MOMRI Affiliate Dr. Fiona Magowan, for her generous hospitality at QUB. Check out our full report and photos here!

New Research and Opportunities

Building on the discussions in Belfast, MOMRI is considering how our research can contribute more effectively to understanding music's relationships to peace and conflict. In recent months, we have participated in several research grant applications with international collaborators, exploring opportunities for the coming years. While these proposals remain under consideration, they reflect our commitment to developing rigorous, interdisciplinary research that can also inform and support the work of practitioners.

We would love to hear your perspectives as we consider our research program in the new year. What kinds of evidence would help us better understand when, how, and under what conditions music contributes to peacebuilding? What important questions remain unanswered, particularly for those working directly in communities affected by conflict? Whether you are a researcher, practitioner, or otherwise interested in this topic, we welcome your thoughts and experiences. Please feel free to reply to this newsletter or contact MOMRI through the Hub.

New MOMRI Talk: Michael Spencer on Active Listening

We're pleased to share a new installment of MOMRI Talks on YouTube, featuring a conversation between MOMRI Director Olivier Urbain and British music educator Michael Spencer. Michael has recently published the Japanese-language book From Hearing to Listening: Active Listening, seeking to make the experience of music performances more accessible to the general public. In this video, he and Olivier apply these active listening practices to a performance by the Kenya National Dancers and Drummers from Min-On's archives. Check out the video here!

New Open-Access Book from MOMRI Affiliate Geoffrey Baker

We congratulate MOMRI Affiliate Dr. Geoffrey Baker on the recent publication of Musicking in a Relational Key: Music Education and Community Music for a Time of Metacrisis (2026). Drawing on his research in Latin America, Geoff critically examines prominent approaches to music education and social action, calling for a renewed focus on relationality. The book offers perspectives relevant to anyone interested in the relationships between music, social change, and human flourishing. The book is freely available, in English, Spanish, and Portuguese.

Welcome to Our Newest MOMRI Affiliate: Benjamin Bergey

We are delighted to welcome Dr. Benjamin Bergey as a MOMRI Affiliate! Benjamin is Associate Professor of Music and Director of Orchestras and Choirs at Eastern Mennonite University in the United States, where he founded an interdisciplinary Music and Peacebuilding program. His work brings together music education, conducting, dialogue, and peacebuilding practice, with particular attention to how shared music-making can foster empathy and relationships across differences.
